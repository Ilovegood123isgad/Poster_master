# Audience and Purpose

Read in stage 1 (brief) and stage 3 (direction). Adapted from the project's audience map and
propagation-goal taxonomy.

## The purpose taxonomy

Choose **one** primary effect. Two secondary effects at most. "Make it look good, attractive,
young, atmospheric" is not an effect — it is a description of a style.

Effects have four layers. Name all four when writing the brief:

| Layer | The question | Typical answers |
|---|---|---|
| Cognitive | What should the viewer know? | What it is, when, where, rules, what changed, who the brand is |
| Emotional | What should they feel? | Curiosity, trust, calm, excitement, warmth, dignity, belonging, urgency |
| Behavioural | What do they do next? | Scan, sign up, buy, attend, consult, comply, donate, share |
| Relational | How should the relationship change? | Stranger → aware → trusting → participating → member |

### Effect → what it demands from the design

| Need | Primary effect | Viewer must know / feel | Action | Verifiable success | Design demand |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Information notice | Accurate understanding | What, when, where, rules | Remember, look it up, comply | Can restate the key info; fewer repeated questions | Clear title, hard information first |
| Event preview | Anticipation and attendance | It is worth coming | Book, buy, sign up, attend | Bookings, attendance rate, inquiries | Key visual carries desire; hard facts stay findable |
| On-site guidance | Fast orientation | Where I am, where to go | Go, queue, check in | Shorter wayfinding, fewer questions | High contrast, arrows, short lines, little decoration |
| Recruitment / joining | "This fits me" | What I gain, what it costs | Apply, join, interview | Applications, valid rate, target-group share | Real people, real scenes, clear path in |
| Partner recruitment | Professional trust and leads | Resources, capability, value | Consult, apply, visit | Valid leads, conversion | Evidence, cases, data, stable structure |
| Product sales | Value understood fast | What it is, who for, why worth it | Buy, trial, visit | Sales, scans, offer redemptions | Product or price as the anchor; direct CTA |
| Service / booking | Lower the barrier | What problem it solves, how to get it | Call, scan, book | Bookings, valid enquiries, conversion | Applicable audience, offer, trust evidence |
| Brand awareness | Be remembered | Who they are, what they mean | Follow, search, share | Search, impressions, recall | Consistent brand assets, no crowding |
| Brand trust | Reduce doubt | Reliable, professional, real | Learn more, try | Feedback quality, trust shift | Credentials, people, data, restrained voice |
| Education | Understand complexity | Why it matters, how it works | Learn, complete, read on | Accuracy, completion, fewer questions | Conclusion first, then diagram and steps |
| Behaviour advocacy | Change behaviour | Why, and exactly what to do | Sort, save, wear, join | Behaviour rate, participation, violations | One instruction, action graphic, immediate benefit |
| Public warning | Avoid error under pressure | Risk, must / must not | Stop, detour, evacuate, ask for help | Recognition speed, compliance, incidents | Very high legibility, no ambiguity |
| Donation / volunteering | Real empathy, low barrier | Who needs help, what my part does | Donate, volunteer, share | Amount, sign-ups, shares | Real story, transparent use, clear entry |
| Community belonging | "This is my group" | My relation to this group | Join, interact, return | New members, return rate | People, shared symbols, participation scenes |
| Festival / anniversary | Emotion and ritual | Worth celebrating, sharing | Attend, share, gift | Shares, attendance, conversion | Key visual and mood first, information light |
| Exhibition / culture | Interest and understanding | Valuable and worth seeing or reading | Buy, visit, read, share | Tickets, visits, dwell time, shares | Concept key visual, narrative layering, whitespace |
| Differentiated launch | Be noticed and discussed | What is different here | Click, try, discuss | Impressions, mentions, trials | Distinctive hook, but keep the entry point |
| Feedback solicitation | Have a voice that counts | My opinion matters and does something | Vote, comment, submit | Valid responses, completion, quality | Purpose, anonymity, deadline, entry |

## The audience profile

Age alone is not an audience. Six dimensions do the work:

1. **Role** — what they are to this event or brand (first-time visitor, member, decision maker).
2. **Motive** — why they would care (belonging, skill, price, curiosity, duty).
3. **Objection** — what stops them (cost, time, effort, risk, social awkwardness).
4. **Attention state** — what they are doing when they see it (walking past, scrolling,
   waiting in line, sitting down).
5. **Decision speed** — seconds, minutes, days.
6. **Context** — with whom, in what mood, in what light.

Common high-frequency intersections:

| Group | What they need most | Typical failure |
|---|---|---|
| First-year students | Belonging, low risk, "will I fit in?" | Posters that only show senior members |
| Parents | Safety, cost, supervision, proof | Posters that omit logistics |
| Professionals | Relevance to their time and level | Student-event aesthetics |
| Parents of young children | Trust, warmth, clarity | Overly cute or over-loud |
| Seniors / older viewers | Legibility, calm, clear instruction | Low contrast, small type, dense copy |
| Passers-by in motion | One idea in one second | Any layout that needs reading |

Mark psychological traits as **creative hypotheses to validate**, never as facts. "Students
prefer lively colours" is a hypothesis; "this audience responds to X" is a finding, and only
after it was actually observed.

## From purpose and audience to style

Do not pick a style first. Pick the style **after** two questions:

1. **What is the information task?** Notify, attract, guide, persuade, belong, warn. The task
   sets the information density and how prominent the CTA is.
2. **What is the audience's psychological state?** Moving, curious, sceptical, loyal,
   indifferent. It sets contrast, pace, and tone.

Then choose the style family that serves both. When the task is *notify* and the audience is
*moving*, information density is low, contrast is high, and the style is quiet. When the task
is *attract* and the audience is *curious*, the key visual leads and the information sits in a
stable block.
