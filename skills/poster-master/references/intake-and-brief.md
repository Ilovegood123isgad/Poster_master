# Intake and Brief

Read this before stage 1 of `../workflows/new-poster.md`.

## The intake table

Fill this before any artwork exists. Each cell is **known** (the client stated it), **assumed**
(you inferred it and will state it), or **missing** (nobody knows yet).

| # | Question | State | Why it matters | Who answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is this poster for, and where will it physically appear? | | Decides size, contrast, and how much text survives | Client |
| 2 | Who is looking at it, and who decides? | | First viewer ≠ payer; the viewer drives the design | Client |
| 3 | What is the single thing this poster must make happen? | | One primary effect; two means none | Client |
| 4 | What are the exact words? | | Facts, not paraphrase. Titles, dates, prices, contacts. | Client |
| 5 | Which assets exist, and what may we do with them? | | Rights state gates every asset | Client |
| 6 | What sizes and files must be delivered, and by when? | | One artwork is not one delivery | Client |
| 7 | Is anything forbidden — a colour, a competitor's mark, a claim? | | Cheapest to learn now | Client |
| 8 | Does it need a real person, a real product, a real place? | | Decides whether generation is even allowed | Client |

## Question order

Ask in this order. Early questions change the whole design; late questions only change details.

1. **Goal** — "What should happen after someone sees this?"
2. **Audience** — "Who is it for? What are they doing when they see it?"
3. **Content** — "Give me the exact text, and where it comes from."
4. **Evidence and boundaries** — "What must not be altered? What may not be shown?"
5. **Delivery** — "Where does it go, what size, which formats, by when?"

## Known, assumed, missing

The distinction is the whole point of intake.

| State | Definition | How it may be used |
|---|---|---|
| Known | Stated by the client and confirmed back to them | May be printed |
| Assumed | Inferred from context, and written down as an assumption | May be used in a draft, labelled in the file; must be confirmed before delivery |
| Missing | Nobody knows | May not be filled in by you. It becomes an open question |

**Never** fill a missing date, price, venue, phone number, QR link, sponsor name, or a client's
product or safety claim with something plausible. A poster that is wrong is worse than a
poster that is late, and the error is invisible until someone is standing in the wrong place.

## Reading the client's own text

Before asking anything, read everything the client sent — full text, not a summary. Extract:

- **Contradictions** — two different dates, two different prices, "signed up" and "registration
  closes" disagreeing.
- **Omissions** — no time, no venue, no contact, no audience definition.
- **Stale information** — last year's date, an event that already happened, a QR for a closed
  group, a price that was a launch offer.
- **Unstated assumptions** — "just make it look good" without saying for whom.
- **Legal or safety exposure** — health, finance, food, education, safety, donation, or
  performance claims that need evidence or a disclaimer.

Each becomes a row in the open-questions table with an owner and a deadline. The client sees
the list; it is not a private note.

## Brief acceptance

The brief is frozen when all of these are true:

- The primary effect is **one** sentence, and it names an audience, a behaviour, and a context.
- The non-goals are written down, so the goal does not expand mid-project.
- The core message fits in one sentence that a stranger could repeat.
- The three things to remember are listed, in priority order.
- Success criteria are observable — a number, a time, or a countable behaviour.
- The carrier, size, and viewing distance are named.
- The deliverable list is written, per version.
- Open questions are listed with owners, not hidden in the prose.

## Common failure patterns

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "Make it more attractive" produces an unusable poster | No primary effect, no audience | Reopen stage 1 before touching style |
| Every element is important | No priority tags | Tag P0/P1/P2 in stage 2 |
| The client rejects the draft for no stated reason | Success criteria were never written | Ask them to pick between two concrete options |
| A fact is wrong in the final file | Facts were edited during design | Re-verify copy after every copy edit |
| The poster looks good on screen and fails on the wall | Carrier was assumed | Test at actual size and distance |
